## 1. Research Context and Motivation

I am an independent software engineer, systems architect, and researcher with approximately 28 years of professional experience in software engineering and systems architecture.

My current research focuses on the engineering relationship between humans and artificial intelligence systems, particularly the preservation of human authority, authorization boundaries, evidence, accountability, and meaningful human control in AI-assisted engineering activities.

I am developing BAES (Babak AI Engineering Standard), an independent, technology-neutral standard for Human–AI Engineering Interaction.

The underlying motivation is that the increasing technical capability of AI systems does not, by itself, establish their authority to make or execute engineering decisions.

A system may produce a technically correct result while exceeding the scope of what a human actually authorized. Similarly, approval of an engineering artifact does not necessarily demonstrate informed authorization of every semantic change incorporated into it.

This creates an important research problem: how can human authority and authorization be represented, preserved, examined, and evidenced throughout AI-assisted engineering activities?

## 2. The Research Problem

The research addresses the distinction between technical execution and authorized execution.

Its central areas of investigation include:

- **Human Authority:** How human decision-making authority is established and preserved.
- **Authorization and Delegation:** How permission to perform engineering activities is defined, limited, and evidenced.
- **Semantic Deviation:** How changes introduced by AI systems can depart from the original human intent, even when they appear technically correct.
- **Evidence and Traceability:** What records are necessary to reconstruct decisions, authorization, and engineering actions.
- **Human Oversight and Override:** How meaningful human control can be maintained throughout delegated AI-assisted activities.
- **Engineering Accountability:** How responsibility and decision provenance can remain identifiable across human–AI interactions.

A central research distinction is:

**Technical Correctness ≠ Authorized Execution**

The research does not assume that every AI-generated deviation is necessarily harmful or unauthorized. Rather, it investigates what evidence is required to establish whether a deviation was within the authority actually granted.

## 3. BAES: Conceptual Foundation and Current Development

BAES is being developed as a technology-neutral engineering standard for Human–AI Engineering Interaction.

Its conceptual architecture distinguishes several areas, including:

- Human Intent
- Human Authority
- AI Reasoning
- Delegated Execution
- Evidence
- Decisions and Traceability
- Governance
- Human Override

The Foundation Baseline, BAES-FND-001 v1.0, has been established and frozen as the current foundational baseline.

Current work is deliberately moving beyond foundational expansion toward experimental Engineering Models, structured research, and evidence-based evaluation.

An important distinction is maintained between:

1. Established foundational concepts.
2. Experimental engineering models and their proposed mechanisms.
3. Research hypotheses and questions that remain unvalidated.

BAES is independently developed. Its intellectual ownership and research independence are to remain clear, while academic criticism, independent evaluation, and collaborative research are welcomed.

## 4. Experimental Engineering Research

An important part of the current research is the independent investigation of human authorization and semantic deviation in AI-assisted engineering.

### 4.1 Human Authorization and Semantic Deviation

The public research project, *Evaluating Human Authorization and Semantic Deviation in AI-Assisted Engineering*, investigates the following question:

**When an AI system changes engineering behavior beyond the scope explicitly authorized by a human, what evidence is sufficient to establish that the deviation was knowingly authorized?**

The current active phase examines the documentary sufficiency, traceability, and reconstructability of controlled evidence packets.

The research uses constructed engineering scenarios to distinguish between:

- Explicit authorization of a specific deviation.
- Approval of an engineering artifact without sufficient evidence of authorization for that deviation.
- Technical correctness and the separate question of authorization.

The current methodology includes evidence reconstruction, criterion-based analysis, provenance examination, and the identification of unresolved ambiguities.

A subsequent research phase is planned to investigate how independent human reviewers interpret the same evidence packets and whether they distinguish artifact approval from informed authorization.

**Current limitation:** The active phase is an investigator-side documentary assessment. Human reviewer validation has not yet been conducted. The findings are not presented as proof of human-review performance, general effectiveness, or BAES superiority.

### 4.2 External Framework and Regulatory Evidence Research

A complementary research direction examines how external engineering, AI governance, and regulatory requirements can be analyzed in relation to human authority, authorization, evidence, and engineering accountability.

The work includes structured examination of relevant public frameworks and the development of experimental Engineering Models for examining requirements and evidence.

The current research program includes:

- NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 analysis.
- Research into ISO/IEC 42001:2023, subject to appropriate access to its authoritative text.
- Academic literature on authority, delegation, control, assurance, and responsibility.
- Engineering scenarios for investigating authorization and evidence boundaries.

An illustrative scenario concerning AI-assisted CV ranking and automated archiving is being prepared for experimental Engineering Model design.

These activities are research and design work, not regulatory certification, conformity assessment, or evidence of operational validation.

Potential collaboration areas include:

**A. Human–AI Interaction and Human Oversight**

Investigating how human authority, decision-making, and meaningful oversight can be maintained in AI-assisted activities.

Exploring joint research proposals and potential applications for appropriate national or European research funding.
