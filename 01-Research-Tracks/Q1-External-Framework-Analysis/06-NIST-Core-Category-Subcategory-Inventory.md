## 1. Inventory summary

The AI RMF 1.0 Core is organized into four Functions. The tables below contain **19 Categories and 72 Subcategories** as enumerated in the published Core.

| Function | Categories | Subcategories | Printed source pages |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| GOVERN | 6 | 19 | 22–24 |
| MAP | 5 | 18 | 26–28 |
| MEASURE | 4 | 22 | 29–31 |
| MANAGE | 4 | 13 | 32–33 |
| **Total** | **19** | **72** | **22–33** |

These counts describe the Core's structural inventory. They are not ratings, weights, priorities, or an indication that every organization must apply every subcategory.

## 2. Reading rules

- NIST states that the Core provides outcomes and actions; the actions are not a checklist and are not necessarily ordered.
- GOVERN is cross-cutting and informs MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE.
- The functions are intended to be iterative and applied in a manner appropriate to the framework user's context, needs, resources, and capabilities.
- The descriptions below are concise researcher paraphrases, not quotations. The source PDF remains authoritative.
- A subcategory appearing in this inventory does not establish implementation or effectiveness.

## 3. GOVERN — 6 Categories / 19 Subcategories

### GOVERN 1 — Organizational policies and practices for AI risk management are established, transparent, and implemented.

- **GOVERN 1.1:** Understand, manage, and document applicable legal and regulatory requirements involving AI.
- **GOVERN 1.2:** Integrate trustworthy-AI characteristics into organizational policies, processes, procedures, and practices.
- **GOVERN 1.3:** Determine the required level of risk-management activity according to organizational risk tolerance.
- **GOVERN 1.4:** Establish transparent risk-management processes and outcomes through policies, procedures, and controls reflecting organizational risk priorities.
- **GOVERN 1.5:** Plan ongoing monitoring and periodic review; clearly define organizational roles and responsibilities, including review frequency.
- **GOVERN 1.6:** Maintain an inventory of AI systems and resource it according to organizational risk priorities.
- **GOVERN 1.7:** Establish safe AI-system decommissioning and phase-out processes that do not increase risk or reduce organizational trustworthiness.

### GOVERN 2 — Accountability structures empower, assign responsibility to, and train appropriate teams and individuals.

- **GOVERN 2.1:** Document and communicate clear roles, responsibilities, and communication lines for AI risk-management activities.
- **GOVERN 2.2:** Provide personnel and partners with training needed to perform AI risk-management duties consistently with relevant policies, procedures, and agreements.
- **GOVERN 2.3:** Assign executive leadership responsibility for decisions about risks associated with AI-system development and deployment.

### GOVERN 3 — Workforce diversity, equity, inclusion, and accessibility are prioritized in AI risk management.

- **GOVERN 3.1:** Inform lifecycle risk-management decisions through diverse teams with varied demographic, disciplinary, experiential, and expertise backgrounds.
- **GOVERN 3.2:** Define and differentiate human-AI roles and responsibilities, including AI-system oversight, through policies and procedures.

### GOVERN 4 — Organizational teams support critical thinking and communication about AI risk.

- **GOVERN 4.1:** Foster critical-thinking and safety-first practices in AI design, development, deployment, and use.
- **GOVERN 4.2:** Document risks and potential impacts of AI technologies and communicate those impacts more broadly.
- **GOVERN 4.3:** Establish practices enabling AI testing, incident identification, and information sharing.

### GOVERN 5 — Processes support engagement with relevant AI actors.

- **GOVERN 5.1:** Collect, consider, prioritize, and integrate feedback from people outside the development/deployment team about potential individual and societal impacts.
- **GOVERN 5.2:** Establish mechanisms for teams to incorporate adjudicated feedback from relevant AI actors into system design and implementation.

### GOVERN 6 — Third-party and supply-chain AI risks are addressed.

- **GOVERN 6.1:** Address risks associated with third parties, including potential infringement of intellectual-property or other rights.
- **GOVERN 6.2:** Establish contingency processes for high-risk failures or incidents involving third-party data or AI systems.

## 4. MAP — 5 Categories / 18 Subcategories

### MAP 1 — Context is established and understood.

- **MAP 1.1:** Understand and document intended purposes, beneficial uses, context-specific laws and norms, prospective deployment settings, users, impacts, assumptions, limitations, and relevant TEVV/system metrics.
- **MAP 1.2:** Ensure interdisciplinary AI actors have suitable competencies and capacities, reflect demographic and domain/user diversity, and document their participation.
- **MAP 1.3:** Understand and document the organization's mission and relevant goals for AI technology.
- **MAP 1.4:** Clearly define or reassess the business value or context of business use.
- **MAP 1.5:** Determine and document organizational risk tolerances.
- **MAP 1.6:** Elicit and understand system requirements from relevant AI actors, considering socio-technical implications in design decisions.

### MAP 2 — The AI system is categorized.

- **MAP 2.1:** Define the specific tasks and methods the AI system will support.
- **MAP 2.2:** Document system knowledge limits and how outputs may be used and overseen by humans, with sufficient information to support decisions and subsequent actions.
- **MAP 2.3:** Identify and document scientific-integrity and TEVV considerations, including experimental design, data selection/collection, trustworthiness, and construct validation.

### MAP 3 — Capabilities, intended use, goals, benefits, and costs are understood.

- **MAP 3.1:** Examine and document potential benefits of intended system functionality and performance.
- **MAP 3.2:** Examine and document potential monetary and non-monetary costs arising from AI errors, functionality, or trustworthiness in relation to organizational risk tolerance.
- **MAP 3.3:** Specify and document the targeted application scope based on system capability, context, and system categorization.
- **MAP 3.4:** Define, assess, and document operator/practitioner proficiency processes and relevant technical standards or certifications.
- **MAP 3.5:** Define, assess, and document human-oversight processes in accordance with organizational GOVERN policies.

### MAP 4 — Risks and benefits are mapped for all system components, including third-party software and data.

- **MAP 4.1:** Establish, follow, and document approaches for mapping technology and legal risks of system components, including third-party data/software and rights infringement.
- **MAP 4.2:** Identify and document internal risk controls for AI-system components, including third-party AI technologies.

### MAP 5 — Impacts on individuals, groups, communities, organizations, and society are characterized.

- **MAP 5.1:** Identify and document likelihood and magnitude of beneficial and harmful impacts using expected use, comparable past systems, public incident reports, external feedback, or other data.
- **MAP 5.2:** Establish and document personnel and practices for regular engagement with relevant AI actors and integration of feedback about positive, negative, and unanticipated impacts.

## 5. MEASURE — 4 Categories / 22 Subcategories

### MEASURE 1 — Appropriate methods and metrics are identified and applied.

- **MEASURE 1.1:** Select approaches and metrics for significant mapped risks; document risks or trustworthiness characteristics that cannot or will not be measured.
- **MEASURE 1.2:** Regularly assess and update metric appropriateness and control effectiveness, including error reports and potential impacts on affected communities.
- **MEASURE 1.3:** Involve internal experts who were not front-line developers and/or independent assessors in regular assessments; consult relevant external experts, users, AI actors, and affected communities as appropriate.

### MEASURE 2 — AI systems are evaluated for trustworthy characteristics.

- **MEASURE 2.1:** Document test sets, metrics, and details of tools used in TEVV.
- **MEASURE 2.2:** Ensure human-subject evaluations meet applicable protections and represent the relevant population.
- **MEASURE 2.3:** Measure performance or assurance criteria qualitatively or quantitatively, demonstrate them under deployment-like conditions, and document the measures.
- **MEASURE 2.4:** Monitor functionality and behavior of AI systems and their components in production.
- **MEASURE 2.5:** Demonstrate validity and reliability and document limits on generalization beyond development conditions.
- **MEASURE 2.6:** Regularly evaluate safety; demonstrate residual negative risk within tolerance and safe-failure capability; use safety metrics covering reliability, robustness, monitoring, and response.
- **MEASURE 2.7:** Evaluate and document AI-system security and resilience.
- **MEASURE 2.8:** Examine and document risks concerning transparency and accountability.
- **MEASURE 2.9:** Explain, validate, and document the AI model; interpret system outputs in context to inform responsible use and governance.
- **MEASURE 2.10:** Examine and document AI-system privacy risk.
- **MEASURE 2.11:** Evaluate fairness and bias and document results.
- **MEASURE 2.12:** Assess environmental impact and sustainability of AI-model training and management.
- **MEASURE 2.13:** Evaluate and document the effectiveness of TEVV metrics and processes.

### MEASURE 3 — Mechanisms track identified AI risks over time.

- **MEASURE 3.1:** Establish approaches, personnel, and documentation to identify and track existing, unanticipated, and emerging risks using intended and actual performance and contextual factors.
- **MEASURE 3.2:** Consider risk-tracking approaches where risks are difficult to assess or suitable metrics are unavailable.
- **MEASURE 3.3:** Establish feedback processes for end users and affected communities to report problems and appeal system outcomes, and integrate them into evaluation metrics.

### MEASURE 4 — Feedback on measurement efficacy is gathered and assessed.

- **MEASURE 4.1:** Connect risk-measurement approaches to deployment contexts, consult domain experts and end users, and document the approaches.
- **MEASURE 4.2:** Use relevant AI-actor input to validate whether trustworthiness results in deployment contexts and across the lifecycle are consistent with intended performance; document results.
- **MEASURE 4.3:** Identify and document measurable performance improvements or declines using consultation with relevant actors and field data about context-relevant risks and trustworthiness.

## 6. MANAGE — 4 Categories / 13 Subcategories

### MANAGE 1 — Risks identified through MAP and MEASURE are prioritized, responded to, and managed.

- **MANAGE 1.1:** Determine whether the AI system achieves intended purposes and stated objectives and whether development or deployment should proceed.
- **MANAGE 1.2:** Prioritize treatment of documented AI risks according to impact, likelihood, and available resources or methods.
- **MANAGE 1.3:** Develop, plan, and document responses to high-priority risks; options include mitigation, transfer, avoidance, or acceptance.
- **MANAGE 1.4:** Document negative residual risks to downstream acquirers and end users.

### MANAGE 2 — Strategies maximize benefits and minimize negative impacts.

- **MANAGE 2.1:** Consider resources and viable non-AI alternatives to reduce the magnitude or likelihood of potential impacts.
- **MANAGE 2.2:** Establish and apply mechanisms to sustain the value of deployed AI systems.
- **MANAGE 2.3:** Follow procedures to respond to and recover from previously unknown risks when identified.
- **MANAGE 2.4:** Assign and clarify responsibilities for superseding, disengaging, or deactivating systems whose performance or outcomes conflict with intended use.

### MANAGE 3 — Risks and benefits from third parties are managed.

- **MANAGE 3.1:** Regularly monitor risks and benefits from third-party resources and apply/document risk controls.
- **MANAGE 3.2:** Monitor pre-trained models used in development as part of regular AI-system monitoring and maintenance.

### MANAGE 4 — Risk treatments and response/recovery/communication plans are documented and monitored.

- **MANAGE 4.1:** Implement post-deployment monitoring plans covering user/actor input, appeal and override, decommissioning, incident response, recovery, and change management.
- **MANAGE 4.2:** Integrate measurable continual-improvement activities into system updates, with regular engagement of interested parties and relevant AI actors.
- **MANAGE 4.3:** Communicate incidents and errors to relevant AI actors and document processes for tracking, responding to, and recovering from them.

## 7. Initial structural observations (not evaluative conclusions)

1. GOVERN is not simply one sequential phase among four; NIST describes it as cross-cutting and infused throughout the other three functions.
2. MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE are linked through iterative risk-context identification, measurement, prioritization, response, and monitoring.
3. Human oversight appears in more than one native location, including GOVERN 3.2, MAP 2.2, MAP 3.5, MEASURE 3.3, and MANAGE 4.1. These references serve different functions and must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated “human control” label.
4. Responsibility for AI-related risk decisions is explicitly addressed at organizational leadership level in GOVERN 2.3. This is not, without further textual analysis, equivalent to a universal task-level approval rule for every AI-assisted engineering action.
5. Documentation, evaluation, feedback, appeal, and override are distributed across the Core rather than appearing as a single isolated evidence function.
6. NIST describes the Core as outcomes and actions, not a checklist or necessarily ordered procedure. Any comparison must preserve that design characteristic.
