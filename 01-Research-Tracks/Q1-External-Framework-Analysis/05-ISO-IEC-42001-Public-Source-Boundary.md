# Q1 — ISO/IEC 42001:2023: Public-Source Boundary Note

**Status:** Initial source observations; not a clause analysis  
**Sources:** Q1-ISO-001 and Q1-ISO-002  
**Access date:** 2026-11-07  
**Protocol:** Q1 Protocol v1.0

## 1. Document identity

**[EXT]** ISO's official catalogue identifies ISO/IEC 42001:2023 as an International Standard, Edition 1, published in December 2023, titled *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system*.

**[EXT]** The IEC Webstore identifies the same publication as ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Edition 1.0, published 2023-12-18, with a length of 51 pages.

## 2. Stated purpose and scope

**[EXT]** ISO and IEC public product descriptions state that the document specifies requirements and provides guidance for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an AI management system within an organization.

**[EXT]** The public descriptions identify intended users as organizations providing or using products or services that utilize AI systems, and state applicability regardless of organization size, type, or nature.

**[INF — limited]** The public descriptions support characterizing ISO/IEC 42001 as an organization-context management-system standard. They do not support a detailed account of individual normative clauses or the exact allocation of requirements among clauses.

## 3. What the public sources do and do not establish

The inspected official public records establish:
- document identity and edition;
- publication date and page count;
- technical committee identification;
- the broad AIMS purpose and organizational context;
- the existence of an official ISO Online Browsing Platform preview entry.

The inspected material does **not** establish:
- the complete wording of the final normative clauses;
- clause-by-clause requirements concerning human authority, task-level approval, delegation, or software change authorization;
- the detailed structure or exact requirements of each clause;
- conformity of any organization or BAES with the standard.

## 4. Source handling rule

The ISO catalogue and IEC product pages are official publication records and public summaries, not substitutes for the licensed normative text. Public-facing benefit statements and FAQ answers must be attributed to the relevant ISO page and must not be presented as verbatim normative requirements.

No FDIS crosswalk or secondary clause summary may be treated as exact final-edition clause text. Any secondary material used later must be separately registered, dated, and explicitly labelled secondary.
