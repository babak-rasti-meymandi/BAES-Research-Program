# Q8 — Engineering Model Design Patterns

**Status:** Proposed  
**Activation:** Not authorized  
**Repository:** BAES-Research-Program  
**Record type:** Exploratory research track

## 1. Purpose

Examine reusable analytical patterns that may help describe, compare, test, and reason about the structure and behavior of engineering models. The track is exploratory: it does not establish BAES requirements, amend the BAES Foundation, or presume that any candidate pattern is necessary or valid.

## 2. Initial research question

Which general-purpose analytical patterns can improve the precision, boundary clarity, dependency reasoning, correction handling, semantic mapping, governance analysis, use-case classification, adversarial evaluation, and interpretation of feedback in engineering models?

## 3. Candidate work packages

1. Canonical definitions and boundary registers.
2. Component relationships and dependency maps.
3. Correction lifecycle and authorization boundaries.
4. Cross-domain semantic mapping.
5. Layered governance analysis.
6. Use-case classification.
7. Structural adversarial evaluation.
8. Feedback interpretation and decision-state transitions.

The candidate topics are listed in the Exploratory Idea Register. Their inclusion is not a finding that they should be combined into one architecture.

## 4. Scope boundaries

- Analyze each candidate independently before considering relationships among candidates.
- Do not treat descriptive patterns as governance authority, authorization, or implementation permission.
- Keep Human Authority, delegated execution, responsibility, evidence, and decision status conceptually distinct.
- Transfer uncertainty only across dependencies that are established and relevant; do not propagate it through assumed associations.
- Do not expand, reinterpret, or amend frozen BAES Foundation material through this track.
- Do not duplicate scenario-specific records held in other project repositories.
- Use only material that is public and lawfully usable; do not reproduce restricted or proprietary expression, diagrams, text, or implementation details.
- Do not infer endorsement, equivalence, or integration from conceptual similarity.
