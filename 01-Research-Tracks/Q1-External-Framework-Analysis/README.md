# Q1 — External Framework Analysis

**Status:** Proposed; scoping required. Research has not started under this track.

## Scoping records

- [Prior-work gap and overlap matrix](00-Prior-Work-Gap-Overlap-Matrix.md)
- [Q1 research protocol — draft v0.1](01-Q1-Research-Protocol-Draft-v0.1.md)

## 1. Working question

What can be responsibly established, using publicly accessible and lawfully usable sources, about how selected external AI governance frameworks address human authority, oversight, delegation, intent preservation, evidence, decisions, and traceability—and how do their own structures and purposes affect that analysis?

Initial subjects:
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) 1.0.
- ISO/IEC 42001:2023.

## 2. Why this is not a repeat of the earlier comparison

The repository [Human-AI-Authorization-Evaluation](https://github.com/babak-rasti-meymandi/Human-AI-Authorization-Evaluation) already contains:
- a historical C1–C20 criterion-conditioned mapping;
- NIST AI RMF 1.0 Core analysis and investigator-side calibration;
- S01-specific evidence-sufficiency analysis;
- a documented scope/criterion-dependence review;
- an ISO/IEC 42001:2023 clause-level access limitation, currently treated as unreviewed/access-limited.

Those records are prior work. Q1 must cite them, preserve their original scope, and avoid presenting them as a comprehensive or independent new comparison.

## 3. Required design constraints

### NIST AI RMF 1.0
Use the framework's native structure: Functions, Categories, and Subcategories. Do not force a clause-level method onto NIST. Clearly distinguish the AI RMF Core from the Playbook, profiles, companion resources, and other NIST publications. Any expansion beyond the Core must be separately scoped and recorded.

### ISO/IEC 42001:2023
Use official public information and credible secondary sources that can lawfully be examined. A public catalogue entry, abstract, preview, or crosswalk does not equal access to the complete final standard. Do not claim complete clause-by-clause coverage or conformity assessment. Retain “not assessed / access-limited” where evidence is insufficient.

### Cross-framework comparison
Do not assume equivalent purposes, units, or structures. Compare only clearly defined research dimensions and state where comparison is not valid. Do not infer absence from inaccessible text.

## 4. Non-duplication gate

Before substantive analysis, produce a gap/overlap matrix with these columns:
- research dimension;
- prior artifact and exact location;
- what prior work established;
- prior method and source boundary;
- limitation;
- genuinely unanswered question;
- proposed Q1 treatment (reuse by citation, extend, or exclude).

The gate must explicitly address the C1–C20 criterion provenance limitation and the fact that earlier ratings are investigator-side, not independent validation.

## 5. Proposed outputs

1. Q1 scoping and non-duplication review.
2. Source and access register for each framework.
3. NIST native-structure analysis protocol.
4. ISO public-source analysis protocol and access limitations.
5. Dimension-specific findings, including unknowns and counterevidence.
6. Bounded comparative synthesis, only where comparison is methodologically defensible.

## 6. Exclusions

Q1 will not:
- rate the overall quality or maturity of either framework;
- rank frameworks;
- claim BAES superiority, novelty, adoption, or effectiveness;
- reconstruct confidential BAES specifications;
- use private BAES material as an undisclosed evaluation instrument;
- treat the historical C1–C20 set as a neutral or exhaustive sampling frame;
- infer ISO clause coverage from inaccessible text.

## 7. Start condition

No substantive Q1 coding or framework rating should begin until:
1. the non-duplication matrix is complete;
2. the source boundary and unit of analysis are approved;
3. the method and reporting limits are documented;
4. the Founder explicitly approves the Q1 protocol.
