# Program Research Methodology

**Status:** Initial cross-track baseline; each track must add a fit-for-purpose protocol.

## 1. Purpose

Provide minimum integrity controls across the research program. This document is not a substitute for a track-specific method, preregistration, ethics review, or source-specific interpretation rules.

## 2. Question specification

Every track must state:
- research question(s);
- intended contribution and unit of analysis;
- scope, exclusions, and target population/source universe;
- method and rationale;
- inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- source and data limitations;
- expected outputs;
- known conflicts of interest;
- conditions under which the result will be considered inconclusive.

## 3. Source handling

For each source, record:
- stable identifier;
- title and issuing organization/author;
- edition/version and publication date;
- source type (primary, secondary, empirical, commentary, dataset, or other);
- canonical URL or bibliographic reference;
- access date;
- exact sections/pages/data examined;
- access limitations and permitted-use considerations;
- claims supported by the source.

Do not treat a search-result snippet, abstract, preview, or crosswalk as equivalent to full source text.

## 4. Analysis discipline

Keep separate:
1. what the source or artifact explicitly states;
2. what the researcher observes;
3. the researcher's interpretation;
4. any inference;
5. hypotheses requiring further testing;
6. unknowns and unexamined areas.

Use the evidence-status vocabulary in the Governance and Boundaries document. For every material conclusion, identify the evidence and the scope within which it holds.

## 5. Comparative analysis

Before comparing frameworks, define:
- the purpose of comparison;
- the unit of analysis for each framework;
- the source boundary for each framework;
- whether the comparison is conceptual, structural, textual, operational, or empirical;
- how non-equivalent structures will be handled;
- how missing or inaccessible material will be represented.

Do not force clause-to-clause or category-to-category equivalence where the source structures differ. Report asymmetry rather than concealing it.

## 6. Rating and synthesis safeguards

- Define scales before applying them.
- Preserve criterion wording and version history.
- Do not change ratings to favor a desired conclusion.
- Do not calculate composite scores or rankings without a justified and pre-specified model.
- Keep independent dimensions separate when they measure different constructs.
- Treat absence from examined material as “not found in examined scope,” not proof of absence from the entire framework.

## 7. Validation

Identify the validation type accurately:
- researcher self-check;
- adversarial challenge review;
- independent second-coder review;
- expert review;
- human-subject study;
- operational/field validation.

These are not interchangeable. Record reviewer independence, materials, disagreements, and resolution procedure.

## 8. Reporting

Each report should include:
- question and scope;
- method and source boundary;
- findings with traceable citations;
- counterevidence and alternative interpretations;
- limitations and unknowns;
- deviations from protocol;
- conclusion calibrated to the evidence;
- reproducibility information where lawful and feasible.

Negative, mixed, and inconclusive results are valid outcomes.
